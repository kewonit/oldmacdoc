# Design and behaviour

What OldMacDoc does and why, in detail. The research behind these choices, with sources, is in [`research_notes/`](../research_notes) and [`reports/`](../reports).

- **Shelf, built to match the original 3D Dock** (measured specs in `research_notes/Original glass dock fidelity/`):
  - **Clear Glass** is the 10.5–10.7 shelf: a two-band grey floor, a dark seam, a bright 3 px front line, blue-white blob indicators on the floor, and the receding "zebra" separator before the Trash.
  - **Frosted** is the 10.8 shelf: a smooth cool floor over a blur, a 1 px white line, a 3 px opaque lip with white-bar indicators, and a thin groove separator.
  - **Smoke** is our own dark variant on the 10.8 shelf. The 10.9 bottom Dock wasn't dark.
  - The floor is a trapezoid with a fixed front-corner overhang (`12 + ceil((h − 17) / 2)`), so its ends keep the same slant however wide the dock gets, and it widens with magnification.
  - It's drawn once into a small image whose middle column stretches to the dock's width (`contentsCenter`), so a frame of magnification or sliding only moves layers and nothing is re-rasterized.
- **Icons on the shelf:** icons stand on the floor and never cover the front line. Reflections are 1:1 mirror images clipped at the front line; Leopard's fade toward the front. The fade and the shelf's reflection opacity are baked into each reflection image, and the clip is a plain rectangle, so there are no masks. Each icon has a soft contact shadow. Tiles sit about 1 pt apart, as on the original.
- **Apps arriving and leaving:** an app that opens grows into the dock from nothing, and one that quits shrinks away where it was (0.25 s), while its neighbours slide along, as on the original. One dragged off in a puff goes at once.
- **Magnification:** the formula from Apple's Dock patent (US 7,434,177). Tile edges move by `S·sin(π/2·d/W)`, so the icon under the pointer reaches exactly the magnified size (up to 128 pt) and stays under the pointer, and the row grows evenly on both sides.
- **Running apps and labels:**
  - Running apps show indicators (Leopard blobs or Mountain Lion bars) that keep their size while icons magnify.
  - The launch bounce is a parabolic hop of 0.6× the icon's height and finishes its current hop once the app has launched.
  - Labels use Lucida Grande 14 on a 60% black pill. They appear instantly, switch instantly, and linger 250 ms after the pointer leaves.
- **Clicking and editing:**
  - Click to open an app or bring it forward. A running app is asked to come forward at once, with all its windows, and `NSWorkspace.openApplication` still sends the reopen that brings minimized windows back.
  - Drag to reorder. Drag off the shelf to remove.
  - Drop an app on the shelf to add it.
  - Drop files on an app to open them with it. Option-drop an image to make it that app's icon.
- **Clicks and menus:**
  - Pressing darkens the icon in its own shape.
  - Click-and-hold, right-click or Control-click opens the icon's menu, drawn as the original's: hanging above the icon with a 26 × 13 pt right-angled pointer aimed at it, its left edge near the icon's (research in `reports/Original Dock right click menus.md`). Clear Glass gets Leopard's light menu; Frosted and Smoke get Snow Leopard's charcoal one (black at 90%, a white rim, Lucida Grande 14, a blue gradient selection).
  - It behaves like a menu: press, drag and release on a row, or click and then click a row; arrow keys, Return, Esc and typing to select; submenus open on hover and flip at the screen's edge; long menus scroll. Option turns Hide into Hide Others and Quit into Force Quit while the menu is open. With VoiceOver on, the same menu is shown as a system menu.
  - The rows follow Apple's order: "Application Not Responding" when the app hangs; its windows (✓ the main one, ◆ minimized); the app's own items (Safari's New Window, Music's controls); Options (Keep in Dock, or Remove from Dock when it isn't open; Open at Login; Show in Finder; Assign To; and OldMacDoc's Icon submenu: Retro Finish / Original / Choose Image… / Reset); Show All Windows; Hide; Quit. Finder adds its own items and recent folders and has no Quit (Option gives Relaunch). The Trash has Open and Empty Trash; the separator has hiding, magnification and Dock Settings…. Titles come from the Dock's own strings, so they read as Apple's, in the same language.
  - Read fresh every time, with no flicker: the app's windows (from the app), Open at Login (macOS's login items list) and Assign To (`app-bindings` and the desktops in `com.apple.spaces`; shown with more than one desktop).
  - The app's own items and recent documents can only come from Apple's Dock, which is still running while parked: its menu for that app is opened, read and closed through Accessibility. That puts Apple's menu on screen for a frame or two, at the middle of the edge where Apple's Dock waits, so it's never done when you open a menu. Every app on the dock is read while every display is asleep or the Mac is locked (the lock screen is drawn above Apple's menu). That's asked of macOS before each read, not kept from notifications, which can be missed; a notice that the screen is coming back stops the reading at once, even mid-read. A screen saver that doesn't lock doesn't count. Reads start a second after the screen goes, one app at a time, and an app that doesn't answer for its windows is skipped (its windows couldn't be told from its own items). Each is remembered for running and not running separately, and read again at the next chance once it may have changed: after you've used, opened or quit the app, or after 10 minutes. Until an app has been read, its menu has no items of its own. Nothing avoids the flicker itself: apps don't expose their Dock menu, Apple's helper answers only once its menu is drawn, screen updates can't be paused for other apps, and asking the app directly needs entitlements only Apple's Dock has (tested on macOS 27.0.1).
  - Choosing one of those rows, or changing Open at Login or Assign To, opens Apple's menu to press the same row; the menu is read again in that same opening, so what's remembered stays current as you use it. Apple's Keep in Dock is never used: it's about Apple's own pins.
  - **App items in menus** (settings panel) turns the reading off: menus then have no app items or recent files.
  - Apple's menu still opens when you're looking in two cases: an app that doesn't answer when its menu opens (only Apple's Dock can say it has stopped responding; the menu opens at once and shows Application Not Responding in place when it does, ignoring choices for 0.3 s after its rows change), and choosing one of Apple's rows. Apple's menu takes the keyboard while it's up and hands it back to the app in front, so the dock's menu takes it back rather than closing. Every opening of Apple's menu runs on one background queue, one at a time and 0.4 s after the last closed (Apple's Dock ignores a menu asked for while the last is still closing), so the dock keeps drawing; a menu Apple's Dock shows too late is watched for and closed.
  - OldMacDoc is never the active app, so its menus and stacks get no mouse-moved events of their own: the pointer reaches them from the same monitors that drive the dock's hover, and the highlight follows it frame by frame.
  - The dock stays up while a menu is open, the icon stays darkened, and magnification holds still. Clicks elsewhere go where they were aimed and close the menu.
  - Cmd-click shows the app in Finder. Option-click switches to the app and hides the one you were in. Option-Cmd-click hides all the others.
- **Removing apps:**
  - A pinned app that isn't running goes up in a five-frame puff (our own cloud art) when dragged off.
  - A running app slides back instead, and it leaves once it quits. Unchecking Keep in Dock behaves the same way.
- **Folders (Stacks):** drop a folder on the dock to keep it there. Folders sit after the apps, past the separator, before the Trash; they're kept in `stacks.json`, apart from the apps' `dock.json`. New users get the folders from Apple's Dock (Downloads, usually).
  - The tile is the folder's first three items piled up (Leopard's stack), or the folder's own icon (Display as Folder), and redraws when the folder changes.
  - Click opens it: a **Fan** (up to 15 items on a curve rising from the tile, names in pills to their left, Open in Finder at the top) when they fit, otherwise a **Grid** (a charcoal panel with the pointer, scrolling, subfolders opening in place with Back); or always one of them, or a **List** (the menu, with subfolders as submenus). Items open with a click and can be dragged out.
  - Its menu: Sort by (Name, Date Added, Date Modified, Date Created, Kind; Downloads starts by Date Added), Display as, View content as, Options (Remove from Dock, Show in Finder), Open.
  - Files dropped on a folder move into it (copied from another disk, or with Option). Nothing already there is ever replaced. Folders reorder among themselves; dragged off, they go up in a puff. Leopard's Downloads stack hops when a download finishes.
- **Badges:** the red count or dot an app puts on its icon, drawn as Leopard's glossy oval at the icon's top right; it magnifies and bounces with the icon. Read from LaunchServices once a second for the running apps on the dock only.
- **Trash:** at the end of the shelf. Click to open it; drop files on it to trash them. The full/empty state works without Full Disk Access. **Empty Trash…** confirms first, then asks Finder; macOS asks permission to control Finder the first time, and only then.
- **Retro icon engine:**
  - It picks the source in this order: your own image, then the app's legacy `.icns`, then the system icon.
  - It lifts the gray plate off system icons only when it is confident, evens out sizes, then applies the Core Image finish (Off / Subtle / Full).
  - Results are cached at 512 px in `~/Library/Caches/OldMacDoc`, and kept in memory for the session.
  - Icons look the same on every shelf, so changing the shelf never re-renders them. A new finish swaps the whole row in one crossfade once it's ready.
- **Auto-hide:** bottom edge of the main display. On the first launch it's on if Apple's Dock was hiding automatically, and off if it wasn't (Apple's default).
  - It comes up at once when the pointer pushes on into the edge (a second mouse event with the pointer pinned there) or while dragging. A pointer that just stops on the edge has to rest there for 0.1 s, so clicks at the bottom of a window don't summon it. It never comes up in the bottom corners, which stay free for hot corners. It slides in over 0.15 s and leaves 0.2 s after the pointer does, sliding out over 0.18 s.
  - The window never moves: the dock slides inside it on the display link, so it can reverse mid-slide, and no strip shows on a display stacked below.
  - Space switches hold reveals for 0.4 s. Wake, display changes and a 1 s check make sure it can't stay stuck up.
  - Reduce Motion fades it instead of sliding. It shows for a moment at launch so it can be found, and clicks pass through everywhere except the shelf.
  - While the settings panel is open it stays up, so every change can be seen; it hides 0.5 s after the panel closes.
- **Windows and the dock, with hiding off:** maximised and tiled windows end above the dock, as with Apple's Dock.
  - Apple's Dock is parked hiding, so macOS thinks the bottom of the screen is free: zoom, Fill and tiling size windows to `NSScreen.visibleFrame`, which only Apple's Dock and the menu bar shrink. No API lets another app reserve space (the private `SLSSetDockRectWithOrientation` only reaches apps launched afterwards), and letting Apple's Dock reserve it would put two docks on screen. So once macOS has maximised a window, it's shortened through Accessibility, as uBar, ExtraDock and Docky do. The window fills the screen first and then steps up.
  - What's shortened: a standard, resizable window on the main display whose bottom ends in the dock's band (the full width, up to the resting icons' top, unmagnified as Apple's is) and that also touches the top, left or right of the visible frame: with the same gap as at the bottom when it reaches the bottom of the screen, or from the top when it ends higher up (zoomed while a shorter dock showed). That's Zoom, Fill, a double-click on the title bar, halves and bottom quarters, by keyboard, menu or dragging to an edge. Its top, left and width stay put; with "Tiled windows have margins" it keeps the same gap above the dock. The rules are in `DockClearance`, with tests.
  - What's left alone: full-screen windows (`AXFullScreen`, or on a full-screen Space for apps that don't say so: on a display with a notch their frame is a filled window's), top halves and quarters, windows that were moved rather than resized, windows put near the dock by hand, and a window resized by hand by an edge, even onto the frame macOS gave it. With the menu bar hidden, Fill covers the whole display, so only `AXFullScreen` and the Space tell it from full screen.
  - Zoom it again, or double-click its title bar again, and it goes back where it was. A shortened window no longer looks maximised to macOS, which would only fill it again. A window manager's Maximize (Rectangle, Magnet) on a shortened window un-maximises it once for the same reason.
  - Turning hiding off, launching, allowing Accessibility, a display change, a Space switch and the dock growing shorten the windows already there. Turning hiding on, quitting and the dock shrinking leave them as they are, as Apple's Dock does; one zoom fills the screen again.
  - Fill and tiling animate for about 0.3 s, and macOS puts a tiled window's frame back if it changes within about 0.6 s of the start, so after an animation nothing is done until 0.8 s after it began. A window put straight back anyway (a tile that didn't animate) is shortened again once that hold is over, and still un-zooms to where it was; three times in a row, or a window that keeps changing back and forth, and that app is left alone until it quits (a tiling window manager at work). An app too busy to answer when it launched, or one that became a regular app later, is picked up when it comes forward. Nothing is done while Mission Control is open, nor for 0.4 s after a Space switch.
  - Every Accessibility call for this runs on a thread of its own, so an app that has stopped answering can't hold up the dock's animation. Apps are watched only while hiding is off and Accessibility is allowed.
- **Mission Control and App Exposé:** macOS always draws its own Dock there, even while parked; no setting changes that.
  - While Apple's Dock waits on a free side edge (see below), it shows there only as a thin strip. Ours is the dock at the bottom: the panel is `.stationary`, so it stays on screen, and it comes up for as long as Mission Control is open, even in auto-hide mode, as the real Dock does.
  - When neither side of the main display is free, ours steps aside instead, within about 20 ms of the trigger and ahead of the system's Dock, and comes back only after that Dock has gone.
  - Detection uses WindowServer window events (private SkyLight calls loaded at runtime, no permission) plus one window-list read per frame at most.
  - If those calls ever disappear, the window's `.transient` behaviour and a check before every reveal still keep the two docks apart.
- **Settings panel:** the menu bar icon opens a panel like Control Center's that stays open while you try things; every setting applies as it's chosen.
  - Shelf as three pictures drawn with the real shelf art (`ShelfPreview`), icon finish as a segmented control, Size and Magnification as sliders with readouts (the far left of Magnification is off; the Size slider ends at the largest size that fits the screen), and switches for Hide automatically, Lights for open apps and Open at login (which says when macOS needs approval, and shows errors in place rather than in an alert). Under Mission Control, the Add Desktop button is a Left / Right choice (see Apple's Dock below). About, Check for Updates and Quit are along the bottom.
  - It's a non-activating panel: it takes the keyboard without making OldMacDoc the active app, so the app in front keeps its menus. Esc or ⌘W closes it, as does a click anywhere else (including on the dock), a Space or display change, or sleep. ⌘Q quits.
  - On macOS 27 it opens through the status item's expanded-interface session, so it takes part in menu bar tracking like a menu; earlier, the icon toggles it. It sits under the icon, or at the top right of the screen when the menu bar is too full to show the icon. Liquid Glass on macOS 26 and later, the menu material before.
  - While the Magnification slider is dragged, the dock magnifies at the middle of the shelf so the size can be seen.
  - Opening the app again from Finder or Spotlight opens the panel. Only one copy runs at a time; a copy still quitting (during an update) doesn't count.
- **About:** the icon, the version (selectable), links to the website, GitHub and the release notes, the bundled licences (Acknowledgements) and Check for Updates.
- **App icon:** drawn by `make icon` from the dock's own shelf art (`scripts/icon/render.swift`): a close-up of the Clear Glass shelf, with its striped separator, on graphite. No Apple artwork.
- **Disk image:** the window shows the app and Applications on the Clear Glass shelf, the striped separator between them as in the Dock, an arrow from one to the other and a line of the song. `make dmg-background` draws it (`scripts/dmg/background.swift`) from the same shelf art; `scripts/dmg/settings.py` places the icons to match.
- **Apple's Dock:**
  - Parked while the app runs, and restored on quit, on SIGTERM, and by a detached watchdog if the app crashes or is force-killed.
  - The watchdog starts with default signal handling (the app itself ignores SIGTERM and handles it on a dispatch source), so quitting stops it. The backup records the process that owns it, and a watchdog only restores its own process's backup, so one left from an earlier run can't touch a copy opened straight after.
  - Only one dock is ever on screen. Apple's is parked before ours appears, and ours is gone before Apple's comes back. There is no setting or mode that shows both.
  - ⌥⌘D and System Settings' "Automatically hide and show the Dock" now toggle *this* dock's auto-hide. The Dock announces the change (`com.apple.dock.prefchanged`) and the app re-parks Apple's Dock straight away, before it can slide in. A 2-second check catches silent `defaults` writes.
  - Quitting always restores the settings from before launch, whatever happened in between.
  - Parked means: auto-hide on, a reveal delay of about 28 hours (so resting the pointer on the edge never brings it up), and `no-bouncing`. Without `no-bouncing`, an app asking for attention (a terminal when you switch away from it, for example) makes the hidden Dock push its bouncing icon up next to ours.
  - When a side of the main display has no other display against it, parked also means that edge, the smallest size and no magnification, so its Mission Control appearance is a strip on the side. The edge is chosen again when displays change.
  - Mission Control puts its + (Add Desktop) on the side away from that strip, so the panel's Add Desktop choice picks the edge. Left, the default, parks Apple's Dock on the right, which also keeps it clear of Stage Manager on the left. Right parks it on the left. When another display is against the edge the choice needs, the other free side is used and the panel says why. Changing the choice moves the Dock (one restart once the choice settles; none if it ends where it started).
  - Your own values for every key come back on quit, and older backups are upgraded with the live values, which are still yours.
- **Accessibility:** VoiceOver sees the dock as a list of buttons, each with its menu (VO-Shift-M). Reduce Motion turns off the launch bounce and hover animations and fades stacks in; Reduce Transparency makes menus opaque.
- **Updates:** Sparkle 2.10.1 (pinned), from the appcast on GitHub Releases, checked once a day without asking first (`SUEnableAutomaticChecks`).
  - OldMacDoc is never the active app, so an update found in the background would show behind other windows. With Sparkle's gentle reminders, its alert shows only when it can take focus (just after launch); otherwise the menu bar icon gets a dot and the panel offers the update.
  - Installing quits OldMacDoc the normal way (Apple's Dock comes back for a moment), and the new version parks it again. Debug builds don't update themselves unless `OldMacDocDebugUpdates` is set.

It asks for one permission, **Accessibility**, once on first launch, for each app's windows and its own items in the menus, and to keep maximised windows clear of the dock while it doesn't hide. Without it, everything else works and the menus show the rest. It never asks for Screen Recording or Full Disk Access. Agent apps that never appear in the Dock (Launchpad/Apps, Mission Control) open without a bounce.
