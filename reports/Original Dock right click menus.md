# Draw the pointer, borrow Apple's menu

OldMacDoc can reproduce the original Dock's tile menus closely by splitting the job: it draws the menu itself, and it borrows from Apple's parked Dock only what no public API can supply. From 10.0 through at least 10.12 Sierra, every Dock menu carried a **right-angled pointer about 26×13 px, centred on the icon**, with the menu's left edge near the icon's left edge. Leopard's menu was the light system pop-up with that pointer added, while Snow Leopard introduced a Dock-only **charcoal menu (black at about 80% opacity, white 14 pt Lucida Grande, a translucent white rim, an edge-to-edge blue gradient highlight)** that Apple's 2013 guidelines show unchanged on Mavericks, so Lion and Mountain Lion almost certainly used it too. Today's Dock hands a plain NSMenu to its DockHelper process, which is why the pointer is gone and why no public NSMenu API can draw one: a faithful copy needs a custom non-activating panel that rebuilds tracking, keyboard navigation and menu accessibility, with the native NSMenu kept as a VoiceOver fallback. The contents follow one skeleton in every version: a disabled "not responding" header when the app hangs, the window list (✓ on the main window, ◆ on minimised ones), the app's own items, then Keep/Remove from Dock, Open at Login and Show in Finder (top level in 10.5, inside **Options ▸** from 10.6, joined there by Assign To in Lion), Show All Windows from Lion on, and finally Hide and Quit, which Option turns into Hide Others and Force Quit; Finder, the Trash and the Dock separator each have their own menu. About half of those items work with **zero permissions**, and that half covers nearly the whole 10.5 menu. The window list, each app's own items, per-app recent documents, Show All Windows, and reliably changing Open at Login or Assign To have no zero-permission source, but because OldMacDoc parks Apple's Dock instead of killing it, **one opt-in Accessibility grant lets it read Apple's complete menu with `AXShowMenu` and replay a choice with `AXPress`**, which none of the third-party docks reviewed does.

## A 26×13 pointer marked every Dock menu from 10.0 to Sierra

The pointer is older than Leopard. In 2002 a developer noted that ordinary contextual menus "are just rectangles but if you control-click on the Dock, it's 'pointy'" ([cocoa-dev, 2002](https://lists.apple.com/archives/cocoa-dev/2002/Aug/msg01008.html)). In November 2007, when developers asked for the same look, Apple's Peter Ammon replied: **"No, there is no way to get a Dock-menu appearance"** for `popUpContextMenu`. Rainer Brockerhoff then reported that he and Matt Gemmell had concluded "it's too much work... to emulate all the little NSMenu details... icons, scrolling, submenus, and whatnot" ([cocoa-dev msg00992](https://lists.apple.com/archives/Cocoa-dev/2007/Nov/msg00992.html), [msg01032](https://lists.apple.com/archives/Cocoa-dev/2007/Nov/msg01032.html)). Both statements still describe the problem OldMacDoc faces.

### Leopard was light, and Snow Leopard through Mavericks went charcoal

Leopard gave pop-up menus four rounded corners, and Siracusa illustrated that with the Dock's own Trash menu under a graphite highlight ([Siracusa, Leopard review p.4](https://web.archive.org/web/2012/http://arstechnica.com/apple/reviews/2007/10/mac-os-x-10-5.ars/4)). Measured pixel by pixel from his native-resolution screenshot, the body is light, compositing to about rgb(218,218,218) over a near-black desktop, with a 3–4 px corner radius, 19 px rows, and an edge-to-edge graphite gradient highlight under white text ([dock-trash-menu.jpg](https://web.archive.org/web/20120723092644im_/http://origin.arstechnica.com/reviews/os/mac-os-x-10-5.media/dock-trash-menu.jpg)). Its pointer is about 26–27 px wide at the base and 13 px tall with a 90° apex, filled in the menu colour with no stroke; the tip sits about 7 px above the Trash artwork and the pointer's axis about 23 px in from the menu's left edge.

Snow Leopard then gave "any and all pop-up menus on the Dock—and only on the Dock" a unique look. Siracusa compared it to Copland's Hi-Tech theme, praised the "translucent edges which accentuate the rounded corners", and admitted it "does clash a bit with the default 'slanty, shiny shelf'" ([Siracusa, Snow Leopard review p.4](https://web.archive.org/web/2010/http://arstechnica.com/apple/reviews/2009/08/mac-os-x-10-6.ars/4)). The style arrived in build 10A402 as **"white text on a charcoal background"** ([MacRumors, July 2009](https://www.macrumors.com/2009/07/10/os-x-snow-leopard-10a402-brings-visual-tweaks-and-other-enhancements/)). Siracusa's Safari menu screenshot is the best reference for OldMacDoc's dark shelves ([dock-menus-1.jpg](https://web.archive.org/web/20101206061843im_/http://static.arstechnica.com/20090828/dock-menus-1.jpg)). Solving two wallpaper-and-body pixel pairs gives a fill of rgb(24,24,24) at about 0.8 alpha, with all three channels agreeing within about two levels, which fits plain alpha blending. Around it runs a 1.5–2 px rim of white at about 30% with a 1 px darker inner line, continuing along both sides of the pointer. Corners are about 6 px, rows 19 px, and separator items about 12–13 px tall with a 1 px line of white at about 20%. The highlight is an edge-to-edge blue gradient from about rgb(115,127,240) to rgb(67,84,250). Window rows carry white ✓ and ◆ marks and 16 px window proxy icons before their titles, and submenus reuse the same material without a pointer. The measured cap height of about 10 px matches **Lucida Grande at 14 pt, whose cap height on macOS 27.0.1 is 10.12 pt**. The font still ships at [/System/Library/Fonts/LucidaGrande.ttc](file:///System/Library/Fonts/LucidaGrande.ttc) and loads with `NSFont(name: "LucidaGrande", size: 14)`, as a probe on this Mac confirmed, which corrects the research notes' assumption that the font is gone.

No Lion or Mountain Lion screenshot of a Dock menu turned up, and Siracusa's reviews of both releases are silent on Dock menus. The evidence on either side is strong, though. Apple's guidelines updated in Lion's launch week show a dark, pointed Dock menu ([HIG 2011](https://web.archive.org/web/20110926103021/http://developer.apple.com/library/mac/documentation/UserExperience/Conceptual/AppleHIGuidelines/Menus/Menus.html)), and the edition published on Mavericks launch day shows the iTunes Dock menu, dark with a light rim and a pointer, above a Mavericks Dock ([HIG 2013 figure](https://web.archive.org/web/20131212010816im_/https://developer.apple.com/library/mac/documentation/userexperience/conceptual/applehiguidelines/art/mn_itunesdockmenu_2x.png)). Snow Leopard's measured fill, composited over white, gives a grey of about 68; the 2013 figure measures 65. The material therefore looks unchanged from 10.6 to 10.9, and **10.7–10.8 being dark is a strong inference rather than an observation**. That matters for OldMacDoc. Its Frosted shelf is the 10.8 floor, so it pairs with the charcoal menu, while its Clear Glass shelf spans 10.5–10.7 and so historically sat under both the light menu and the dark one.

### Yosemite shrank the pointer; the Big Sur era likely dropped it

The Yosemite-era guidelines state that "the Dock and Dock menus use the current appearance" and show light and dark vibrant iTunes menus, both pointed, with the pointer measuring about **22×12 pt and its axis about 25 pt in** ([OS X HIG, Dock Menus](https://web.archive.org/web/20160410090049/https://developer.apple.com/library/mac/documentation/UserExperience/Conceptual/OSXHIGuidelines/OtherMenus.html)). A Sierra-wallpaper Messages screenshot in the 2018 guidelines still has the pointer ([macOS HIG 2018](https://web.archive.org/web/20180618155436/https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/macos/menus/dock-menus/)). On macOS 27.0.1 the mechanism is different. The Dock's `DockHelper.xpc` links AppKit and contains `popupWithMenu:direction:rect:dark:orientation:fromKeyboard:accessibilityContainer:withReply:` and NSMenu's private `_popUpMenuRelativeToRect:inView:preferredEdge:`, while the Dock's resources hold no menu-pointer art, only the Stacks grid arrows ([local DockHelper binary](file:///System/Library/CoreServices/Dock.app/Contents/XPCServices/DockHelper.xpc/Contents/MacOS/DockHelper); [Dock resources](file:///System/Library/CoreServices/Dock.app/Contents/Resources/)). **The Dock now hands over a menu, the icon's rect and a "dark" flag, and a real NSMenu appears.** That NSMenu has no callout, uses the spacing and rounded highlight Big Sur introduced ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/stories/macos-big-sur-the-macstories-review/5); [Pixel Envy](https://pxlnv.com/blog/big-sur-design-observations/)), and turns to Liquid Glass from macOS 26 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dock_(macOS))). The exact release that removed the pointer, somewhere from 10.13 to 11.0, could not be pinned down; Big Sur's menu redesign is the likeliest candidate.

The six screenshots that do have a pointer agree on placement. The pointer is centred on the icon and the menu's left edge sits within a few pixels of the icon's left edge, which puts the axis about 23–28 px from the menu's left edge: half the pointer base, plus the corner radius, plus a few pixels. Every reference used icons of about 50 px, so the evidence cannot say whether the real rule was "align to the icon's left edge" or "a fixed offset of about 25 pt". For OldMacDoc's larger and magnified icons the fixed offset is the safer reading, because it falls out of the shape's geometry. **Still unknown** are how the pointer behaved on a side Dock, how the menu flipped or clamped at a screen edge, the open and close animation, and the shadow parameters.

## Only a custom panel can draw the pointer, so it must re-earn NSMenu's behaviour

Four routes exist, and only one reaches the 10.5–10.9 look. **Keeping NSMenu** gives arrow keys, type-select, submenus, scrolling, Option alternates and correct VoiceOver semantics for free, but its chrome cannot be changed: `NSMenuItem.view` restyles rows only, and the header itself notes that "Menu item views are not supported in the Dock menu" ([NSMenuItem.h](file:///Applications/Xcode.app/Contents/Developer/Platforms/MacOSX.platform/Developer/SDKs/MacOSX.sdk/System/Library/Frameworks/AppKit.framework/Headers/NSMenuItem.h); [Apple: NSMenuItem.view](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appkit/nsmenuitem/view)). **NSMenu plus a separate tail window** keeps native behaviour but cannot match the system material, shadow or corner join, and because the menu window's real frame is private, the tail slips whenever AppKit clamps the menu. **NSPopover** has a free arrow and edge flipping, but its arrow and material are modern and fixed, its HUD appearance has been gone since 10.10, and its content is not a menu, so every behaviour still has to be rebuilt ([NSPopover.h](file:///Applications/Xcode.app/Contents/Developer/Platforms/MacOSX.platform/Developer/SDKs/MacOSX.sdk/System/Library/Frameworks/AppKit.framework/Headers/NSPopover.h)). **A borderless, non-activating NSPanel that draws its own shape and rows** is the only route to fidelity. It follows the pattern of MAAttachedWindow, INPopoverController and SFBPopovers, which supply the window and frame drawing but no menu behaviour ([MAAttachedWindow](https://github.com/lowell/MAAttachedWindow); [INPopoverController](https://github.com/indragiek/INPopoverController); [SFBPopovers](https://github.com/sbooth/SFBPopovers)). The research found no maintained package that implements a full callout menu.

### Drawing the shape

OldMacDoc already has the window plumbing. Its Settings panel is a borderless `.nonactivatingPanel` at `.popUpMenu` level whose `canBecomeKey` returns true, so it takes keystrokes without activating the `.accessory` app ([SettingsPanel.swift](file:///Users/kew/Github/oldmacdonal/Sources/App/SettingsPanel.swift)). The menu panel uses the same recipe with `isOpaque = false`, `backgroundColor = .clear` and `hasShadow = true`, and calls `invalidateShadow()` after its first draw so the shadow follows the pointer. The shape is a single path, verified by rendering it on this Mac:

```swift
/// Rounded rect with the pointer on its bottom edge; the tip sits at (axisX, r.minY).
func calloutPath(in r: NSRect, axisX: CGFloat, base: CGFloat = 26, height: CGFloat = 13, radius: CGFloat = 6) -> NSBezierPath {
    let b = NSRect(x: r.minX, y: r.minY + height, width: r.width, height: r.height - height)
    let p = NSBezierPath()
    p.move(to: NSPoint(x: b.minX + radius, y: b.maxY))
    p.appendArc(from: NSPoint(x: b.maxX, y: b.maxY), to: NSPoint(x: b.maxX, y: b.minY), radius: radius)
    p.appendArc(from: NSPoint(x: b.maxX, y: b.minY), to: NSPoint(x: b.minX, y: b.minY), radius: radius)
    p.line(to: NSPoint(x: axisX + base / 2, y: b.minY))
    p.line(to: NSPoint(x: axisX, y: r.minY))
    p.line(to: NSPoint(x: axisX - base / 2, y: b.minY))
    p.appendArc(from: NSPoint(x: b.minX, y: b.minY), to: NSPoint(x: b.minX, y: b.maxY), radius: radius)
    p.appendArc(from: NSPoint(x: b.minX, y: b.maxY), to: NSPoint(x: b.maxX, y: b.maxY), radius: radius)
    p.close()
    return p
}
```

For Clear Glass, fill the path with near-white at about 0.85–0.9 alpha with a 4 px radius and no stroke. For Frosted and Smoke, fill it with rgb(24,24,24) at 0.8 alpha, stroke it inset by half the line width with 1.5–2 px of white at about 0.3, and add a 1 px dark line just inside. The axis must stay at least half the base plus the radius from either end, so near the right edge of the screen the menu flips to extend leftward with the pointer near its right corner. Because the Snow Leopard composites fit plain alpha blending, start without a backdrop blur; if one is wanted later, `NSVisualEffectView.maskImage` clips it to the path and "will correctly influence the window's shadow" ([NSVisualEffectView.h](file:///Applications/Xcode.app/Contents/Developer/Platforms/MacOSX.platform/Developer/SDKs/MacOSX.sdk/System/Library/Frameworks/AppKit.framework/Headers/NSVisualEffectView.h)). When `accessibilityDisplayShouldReduceTransparency` is set, swap the translucent fill for an opaque one.

### Rebuilding tracking and the keyboard

Behaviour is where the cost lies. The tile's `mouseDown` already starts OldMacDoc's 0.5 s hold timer; when it fires, the new code shows the panel and runs `trackEvents(matching: [.leftMouseDragged, .leftMouseUp], ...)`, hit-testing rows in screen coordinates because the dock window that took the mouse-down keeps receiving the drags ([Apple: trackEvents](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appkit/nswindow/trackevents(matching:timeout:mode:handler:))). Releasing over a row chooses it; releasing over the tile, or releasing quickly after a right-click or Control-click, leaves the menu open as a click-to-open menu. Global monitors for mouse-downs elsewhere dismiss it and need no permission, but **global monitors for key events need Accessibility**, according to AppKit's own header ([NSEvent.h](file:///Applications/Xcode.app/Contents/Developer/Platforms/MacOSX.platform/Developer/SDKs/MacOSX.sdk/System/Library/Frameworks/AppKit.framework/Headers/NSEvent.h)), so the panel must become key and handle its own keystrokes rather than watch them from outside. Up and Down skip separators and disabled rows, Right and Left open and close a submenu, Return, Enter and Space choose, Escape closes, and typing selects a row by title with a buffer of about a second. `flagsChanged` flips Hide to Hide Others and Quit to Force Quit live. Options ▸ becomes a second panel with no pointer, opened after a short hover delay, with a diagonal "safe triangle" that stops the pointer from switching rows on its way into the submenu.

The Dock's binary contains `_menuPopupDelay` and `_menuPopupInterval` keys whose default values are unknown ([Dock binary](file:///System/Library/CoreServices/Dock.app/Contents/MacOS/Dock)). OldMacDoc's 0.5 s hold is consistent with Leopard, which opened the menu on click-and-hold ([Lap Cat Software](https://lapcatsoftware.com/blog/2009/08/28/snow-leopard-hidden-dock-preference/)), and with Lion and later, where click-and-hold opens the same full menu as a right-click ([MacRumors forum, 2011](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/lion-osx-show-all-windows-like-snow-leopard.1197752/)). **Only 10.6 differed**: there, holding the click opened that app's windows in Exposé plus a minimal menu ([Apple HIG 2009](https://archive.org/download/apple-hig/MacOSX_HIG_2009_08_20.pdf)), which OldMacDoc has no reason to copy.

### Accessibility is the riskiest part

The panel's content view should take `accessibilityRole = .menu`, with each row an `NSAccessibilityElement` of role `.menuItem` carrying a title, its enabled state and a press action. Apple's Dock reports checkmarks through `AXMenuItemMarkChar`, so each row should expose the same attribute, and the panel should post the HIServices `AXMenuOpened`, `AXMenuClosed` and focus-change notifications, which AppKit has no constants for ([AXNotificationConstants.h](file:///Applications/Xcode.app/Contents/Developer/Platforms/MacOSX.platform/Developer/SDKs/MacOSX.sdk/System/Library/Frameworks/ApplicationServices.framework/Frameworks/HIServices.framework/Headers/AXNotificationConstants.h); [Apple: kAXMenuOpenedNotification](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/applicationservices/kaxmenuopenednotification)). **Apple does not document whether VoiceOver treats a non-NSMenu window exactly like a system menu**, so this needs hands-on testing. The safe design builds every tile menu as an `NSMenu` model and treats the callout panel purely as a renderer of that model; when `NSWorkspace.shared.isVoiceOverEnabled` is true, OldMacDoc shows the native NSMenu instead. Apple's Dock tiles also expose `AXShowMenu` ([local AX test on macOS 27.0.1](file:///System/Library/CoreServices/Dock.app)), and VoiceOver users open Dock menus with VO-Shift-M ([AppleVis](https://www.applevis.com/forum/macos-mac-apps/removing-things-my-dock-big-sur-1123), search excerpt only). OldMacDoc's `TileElement` implements only press today ([DockView.swift](file:///Users/kew/Github/oldmacdonal/Sources/App/DockView.swift)), so adding `accessibilityPerformShowMenu()` is a one-method fix.

Some fidelity is available before any panel exists. OldMacDoc currently centres an NSMenu over the tile ([DockView.swift](file:///Users/kew/Github/oldmacdonal/Sources/App/DockView.swift)); placing the menu so its left edge sits about 25 pt left of the icon's centre matches Apple's placement. `menu.appearance = NSAppearance(named: .darkAqua)` on Frosted and Smoke matches the Dock's own "dark" flag. Setting `NSMenu.font` (available since 10.6) to Lucida Grande 14 brings back the period typeface, where the system menu font measures 13 pt on 27.0.1 ([NSMenu.h](file:///Applications/Xcode.app/Contents/Developer/Platforms/MacOSX.platform/Developer/SDKs/MacOSX.sdk/System/Library/Frameworks/AppKit.framework/Headers/NSMenu.h)).

## Every Dock menu follows one skeleton, top to bottom

Apple's guidelines have said since the Tiger era that an app's custom items "appear above the standard Dock menu items", and that Option turns Hide into Hide Others and Quit into Force Quit ([Apple HIG 2008](https://archive.org/download/apple-hig/MacOSX_HIG_2008_06_09.pdf)). The shipping Dock's template on macOS 27.0.1 encodes the same order, marking those primary and alternate pairs `dynamic=0` and `dynamic=1`, while the Dock's code inserts the window list and the app's own items above them ([DockMenus.plist](file:///System/Library/CoreServices/Dock.app/Contents/Resources/DockMenus.plist)). A 2015 Yosemite copy of that file is almost identical ([2015 plist gist](https://gist.github.com/Darkle/2ca4152ec3cbc90b8aa9)), so the skeleton has been stable for at least a decade, and live menus opened through Accessibility on 27.0.1 confirm it for Safari, Chrome, Music, Notes, Ghostty, Maps, Numbers and Finder.

| Slot, top to bottom | 10.5 Leopard | 10.6 Snow Leopard | 10.7–10.8 Lion, Mountain Lion | macOS 14–27 (verified 27.0.1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hung-app header | Not documented | Not documented | Not documented | "Application Not Responding" (disabled), then a separator |
| Window list | ✓ main, ◆ minimised | ✓, ◆, with 16 px proxy icons | ✓, ◆ | ✓, ◆ |
| App's own items | Between separators | Same | Same | Same; may include submenus |
| Keep/Remove from Dock, Open at Login, Show in Finder | Top level | Inside Options ▸ | Options ▸, plus Assign To with 2+ desktops | Options ▸, plus Assign To |
| Show All Windows | Absent | Unresolved | Present | Present |
| Hide or Show (Option: Hide Others) | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Quit (Option: Force Quit) | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Not running, pinned | Remove from Dock, Open at Login, Show in Finder, ───, Open | Options ▸ (same three), ───, Open | Options ▸, ───, Show Recents (if supported), Open | Recent documents, ───, Options ▸, ───, Open |

"Not documented" in the hung-app row means no source shows the header for 10.5–10.8, not that it was absent.

### Running apps

Safari's live menu on 27.0.1 is the template filled in: `✓ <window title>`, a separator, Safari's own `applicationDockMenu` items **New Window** and **New Private Window**, a separator, **Options ▸** (✓Keep in Dock, Open at Login, Show in Finder, a separator, the Assign To header, All Desktops, This Desktop, ✓None), a separator, then **Show All Windows**, **Hide** and **Quit** (live AX dump; [DockMenus.plist](file:///System/Library/CoreServices/Dock.app/Contents/Resources/DockMenus.plist)). An unpinned running app shows **Keep in Dock unchecked**, and a not-running pinned app shows **Remove from Dock** instead of a checkbox, which TidBITS describes as "Options > Remove from Dock if the app is closed or Options > Keep in Dock if it's open" ([TidBITS 2017](https://tidbits.com/2017/03/06/macos-hidden-treasures-dominate-the-dock/)). Open at Login shows a ✓ when it is on. Assign To appears only when more than one desktop exists ([Cult of Mac](https://www.cultofmac.com/news/assign-applications-to-mission-control-spaces-in-lion-os-x-tips), search excerpt), and its strings include "Desktop on Display %@" for setups with a separate Space per display ([DockMenus.strings](file:///System/Library/CoreServices/Dock.app/Contents/Resources/en.lproj/DockMenus.strings)). Hide becomes **Show** while the app is hidden, and **Open never appears for a running app**. When an app hangs, the menu gains a disabled "Application Not Responding" header, and Apple's own instructions have users choose Force Quit from this menu ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/use-the-dock-mh35859/mac)). Window rows use ✓ for the main window and ◆ for minimised ones ([MacMost 2025](https://macmost.com/get-the-most-from-the-mac-dock-with-dock-menus.html)); the research could not settle how windows are ordered, whether windows on other Spaces are listed, or whether the Dock ever shows the unsaved-changes bullet.

Leopard's version has to be reconstructed. Apple's 2008 guidelines list the windows plus Show In Finder, Hide and Quit, with "Keep In Dock" when the tile is not permanent ([Apple HIG 2008](https://archive.org/download/apple-hig/MacOSX_HIG_2008_06_09.pdf)), and a 2008 user report adds Remove from Dock and Open at Login ([Yahoo Answers](https://answers.yahoo.com/question/index?qid=20081010092039AAwpVua), search excerpt). The best reconstruction is therefore the window list, a separator, the app's own items, a separator, Remove from Dock (or Keep in Dock on an unpinned tile), Open at Login and Show in Finder, a separator, then Hide and Quit. Snow Leopard moved the middle three into the new Options submenu ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/199787/snow-leopard-changes.html), search excerpt; [Deschamps, 2010](https://dennisdeschampsblogs.blogspot.com/2010/09/mac-os-x-v106-mac-101-dock-dock-expose.html)). Lion added Assign To and a Show Recents item for apps that support it ([Apple Community, 2011](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/3258198)); on 27.0.1 an app with recent documents, such as Numbers, lists them inline at the top of its not-running menu instead.

Finder's middle block is not Dock code: it is Finder's own `applicationDockMenu`, so it obeys the same rules as Safari's New Window. The shipping wording uses lowercase linking words ("Show in Finder", "Keep in Dock", "Open at Login", "Remove from Dock"); Apple's guidelines title-case them ("Show In Finder"), but the lowercase forms are the safer choice even for a Leopard replica. Only "Dock Settings…" and Finder's Find…, Go to Folder… and Connect to Server… carry an ellipsis, and documents use curly quotes, as in Open “%@” ([DockMenus.strings](file:///System/Library/CoreServices/Dock.app/Contents/Resources/en.lproj/DockMenus.strings)).

### Where OldMacDoc's menu departs from Apple's

OldMacDoc's app tiles currently offer Open, Show/Hide, Keep in Dock, Show in Finder, Icon ▸ and Quit (Force Quit with Option), and its Trash offers Open and Empty Trash… ([DockView.swift](file:///Users/kew/Github/oldmacdonal/Sources/App/DockView.swift)). Three of those items differ from Apple's: it shows **Open for running apps**, which the Dock never does; it shows a **Keep in Dock checkbox where the Dock says Remove from Dock** on a not-running pinned tile; and it adds an **ellipsis to Empty Trash** that the Dock's string doesn't have. It also lacks the window list, the app's own items, Open at Login, Hide Others, the not-responding state, Finder's commands and the separator menu. OldMacDoc's own Icon ▸ submenu has no Apple counterpart; it fits most naturally after Show in Finder, at the top level in the Leopard layout and inside Options ▸ in the later layouts.

## Zero permissions cover the Leopard menu; Accessibility covers the rest

The hardest items to get are each app's own entries, because nothing outside the Dock can request them. On macOS 27.0.1, AppKit answers the Dock over a private WindowServer "dock message" channel: the private class `NSCGSDockNotifications` has handlers such as `addGetAppMenuHandler:` and `addDoMenuCommandHandler:`, and the Dock holds the private entitlement `com.apple.private.skylight.universal-owner` (local runtime and `codesign` inspection). The SDK defines no Apple Event that fetches a Dock menu, and the documented model is simply that apps hand their menu to the Dock ([Apple: applicationDockMenu](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appkit/nsapplicationdelegate/applicationdockmenu(_:))). **Asking an app for its Dock menu directly is not feasible.** The workaround people actually use is UI scripting of the Dock itself ([Keyboard Maestro forum](https://forum.keyboardmaestro.com/t/accessing-the-dock-menu-of-an-application/8995)).

That workaround is far better than it sounds, because of how OldMacDoc hides the Dock. With the Dock auto-hidden on 27.0.1, `AXUIElementPerformAction(tile, "AXShowMenu")` succeeded at once, and `AXShownMenuUIElement` returned the full menu: Safari's own New Window and New Private Window, the titled window list, every Options child with its ✓ (read from `AXMenuItemMarkChar` without opening the submenu), Show All Windows, Hide, Hide Others, Quit and Force Quit, and for Finder its ten recent folders. `AXCancel` closed the menu, and the tile's position did not change. Running-app tiles also expose `AXShowExpose`, which is App Exposé for that app (local AX test). DockDoor reads Apple's Dock through Accessibility to track hovering but never calls `AXShowMenu` ([DockDoor DockObserver.swift](https://github.com/ejbills/DockDoor/blob/baf6425a981b09acb79189ec568e7c189b70a0ce/DockDoor/Utilities/DockObserver.swift)), and the research found **no third-party dock that proxies apps' own Dock menus**.

The proxy works like this. On a right-click, OldMacDoc finds Apple's tile by `AXURL`, opens its menu, reads it, cancels it, and shows its own menu built from what it read. When the user chooses an item, OldMacDoc reopens Apple's menu and presses the item with the same title and position. It must not proxy Keep in Dock or Remove from Dock, because those refer to Apple's pin list rather than OldMacDoc's. The coverage gap is small: every running app always has a tile in Apple's Dock, so the window list and app items are always reachable, and only recent documents for a not-running app pinned in OldMacDoc but not in Apple's Dock are lost. **Three things are untested**: whether Apple's menu flashes at the parked Dock's edge under OldMacDoc's huge `autohide-delay`, how long the read-and-cancel round trip takes, and whether `AXPress` on items such as Assign To or a window acts at once while the Dock is parked.

Each zero-permission gap has a specific cause. Other apps' window titles have been withheld from `CGWindowListCopyWindowInfo` since Catalina unless Screen Recording is granted, and AltTab confirms that on macOS 27 they appear and vanish the moment the grant changes ([AltTab](https://github.com/lwouis/alt-tab-macos/blob/2a9cbbb5462fbd0a76bb3f5f50d71b357823d6ab/src/macos/SystemPermissions.swift); [Michael Tsai, 2019](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2019/11/22/detecting-screen-recording-permission-on-catalina/)). macOS 14 made `NSRunningApplication.activate` "an advisory 'request'", so AltTab fronts a single window with private SkyLight calls plus Accessibility ([AltTab Window.swift](https://github.com/lwouis/alt-tab-macos/blob/2a9cbbb5462fbd0a76bb3f5f50d71b357823d6ab/src/switcher/state/Window.swift)); pressing the window item in Apple's menu avoids that private code. `SMAppService` can register only the calling app and its own helpers ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/servicemanagement/smappservice)), while the Dock writes login items through the legacy shared-file-list API using private entitlements. A non-entitled probe could read that list without any prompt, but whether writes work is the key open experiment, and one developer reports they did nothing ([shuttle PR #4](https://github.com/truthatt11/shuttle/pull/4)). Per-app recents sit in a folder protected by Full Disk Access, and the deprecated per-app list API returned Xcode's items once and then nothing on later runs, so it cannot be relied on. Writing the Spaces binding for Assign To from outside only takes effect after `killall Dock`, which would undo the parking.

Permission costs vary as much as coverage does. **Accessibility** must be switched on by hand in System Settings, often needs a relaunch before the app detects it, and grants broad control of the user interface ([DockDoor 1.40.2 notes](https://newreleases.io/project/github/ejbills/DockDoor/release/1.40.2)). **Screen Recording** has prompted again every month since Sequoia ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2024/08/15/macos-sequoia-screen-recording-app-permissions/)). **Full Disk Access** is the broadest grant there is. **Automation** consent applies to one sender and one target app, so OldMacDoc's existing on-demand Finder grant for Empty Trash also covers other Finder events, while System Events would mean a second prompt ([HackTricks: Apple Events](https://book.hacktricks.xyz/macos-hardening/macos-security-and-privilege-escalation/macos-security-protections/macos-tcc/macos-apple-events)).

Other docks pay these costs for previews rather than menus. [DockDoor](https://dockdoor.net/) asks for Accessibility and Screen Recording to find windows and show previews. [uBar](https://kelaenderkoe.pages.dev/float-https-ubarapp.com/documentation) uses Accessibility for window control, Screen Recording for titles and previews, and optional Full Disk Access only for the Trash. [ExtraDock](https://extradock.app/docs/permissions.html) uses Accessibility for window state and Screen Recording for thumbnails, and can list apps per Space without Accessibility. None of them documents copying the pointer menus. Elsewhere, elementary's Pantheon dock considered switching to popovers because "the caret pointing to the dock item is another clear signal that the menu applies only to that dock item" ([Launchpad bug 1293356](https://bugs.launchpad.net/pantheon-dock/+bug/1293356)).

## A three-step plan keeps the no-permission promise

OldMacDoc's README promises that it asks for no permissions. The plan below keeps that promise as the default and makes the one useful permission opt-in.

| Step | What ships | Permission | Main open question |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Fix the contents, still in NSMenu | The era skeleton, Apple's wording, the Finder and separator menus, left-aligned placement, the dark appearance, Lucida Grande | None (Finder automation stays on demand) | Whether third-party login-item writes work on 14, 15, 26 and 27 |
| 2. Callout renderer | A custom panel that draws the NSMenu model with the pointer, rim and highlight per shelf | None | How VoiceOver treats a custom menu panel |
| 3. "Use Apple's Dock menus" | Proxied window list, app items, recents, hung state, Assign To and Show All Windows | Accessibility, opt-in | Flashing and latency while the Dock is parked |

**Step 1** costs little and fixes most of the differences a user would notice. The menu's structure should follow the shelf: Clear Glass uses the flat Leopard layout (windows, app items, Remove from Dock or Keep in Dock, Open at Login, Show in Finder, Hide, Quit), and Frosted and Smoke use the Lion and Mountain Lion layout with Options ▸ and Assign To. Apple's Dock leaves out template items it can't support, and OldMacDoc should likewise leave out any item it cannot perform rather than show it disabled, so without Accessibility, Frosted and Smoke drop Show All Windows and both layouts drop the window list and app items. Open should appear only on not-running tiles, which should say Remove from Dock when pinned. Hide Others becomes the Option alternate of Hide, hiding every other regular app. The Open at Login checkmark comes from the session login-items list; whether choosing it writes that list or opens Login Items settings waits on the experiment. Assign To can show its state, but changing it needs Accessibility. Finder gets Find…, New Finder Window under the existing Finder grant, and its recent folders from `FXRecentFolders`, and it has no Quit. The separator gets a menu whose Turn Hiding and Turn Magnification items map to OldMacDoc's settings, whose Position on Screen choices are limited to what OldMacDoc supports, and whose "Dock Preferences…" opens OldMacDoc's own settings panel; Minimize Using belongs to Apple's Dock and should be left out. Empty Trash loses its ellipsis, and the tiles gain `accessibilityPerformShowMenu()`.

**Step 2** replaces how the menu is drawn, not what is in it: the 26×13 pointer, 19 px rows, an edge-to-edge highlight and Lucida Grande 14. Clear Glass gets the light Leopard material, and Frosted and Smoke get the charcoal rim. Clear Glass can offer the charcoal menu as a "Snow Leopard menus" option, since 10.6 and 10.7 used it above the same shelf. The highlight gradient comes from the measured blue, or the graphite one when `controlAccentColor` is graphite. Under VoiceOver the menu falls back to the native NSMenu until testing shows the custom panel is announced correctly.

**Step 3** is a setting, off by default, that asks for Accessibility only when switched on. Before committing to it, test `AXShowMenu` under OldMacDoc's real parking for flashing and round-trip time, and test `AXPress` on window and Assign To items. Three private zero-permission calls could each move a feature into Step 1: `CoreDockSetWorkspacesAppBindings` for Assign To, `CoreDockSendNotification("com.apple.expose.front.awake")` after activating the target for Show All Windows, and third-party `LSSharedFileList` writes for Open at Login. Each could break in any macOS release, so ship them only behind tests that confirm they took effect. Screen Recording, Full Disk Access, System Events automation and anything that restarts the Dock should stay off the table, because **each costs more trust than the Accessibility proxy and covers less**.

## Conclusion

The pointer disappeared at the moment Apple stopped drawing Dock menus itself and started handing a stock NSMenu to DockHelper, so reproducing it means reversing that step inside OldMacDoc: owning the drawing again while keeping NSMenu as both the data model and the accessibility safety net. The fidelity work and the permission budget also line up better than expected. The menus OldMacDoc's Clear Glass shelf imitates are the simplest Apple ever shipped, with no Options submenu, no Assign To and no Show All Windows, so the most authentic layout is also the one that needs the fewest permissions.

The larger strategic finding is that parking Apple's Dock, chosen for crash safety, leaves OldMacDoc with a live, fully accessible menu server. Every other dock reviewed rebuilds window lists through Screen Recording and gives up on apps' own items, while OldMacDoc can show Safari's New Private Window, Music's Rating submenu and Finder's recent folders exactly as Apple's Dock would, for one opt-in grant. The one unknown that could change this plan is whether `AXShowMenu` flashes visibly while the Dock is parked, and testing that comes first.
